# Vision and Usage Guide

## Vision

Meripotens exists to turn scattered reading and thinking across the humanities into a structure that grows sturdier over time instead of more cluttered. Most note-taking systems either impose too much structure too early, which kills the raw thinking before it has a chance to develop, or impose no structure at all, which leaves years of notes unusable. This vault is built around a different idea: structure is earned, one layer at a time, and every note has to justify its promotion to the next layer through the work of writing, connecting, and revisiting.

The five core layers name this process directly. Essence is the ground the system stands on. Power is the raw, sometimes chaotic material that has not yet proven its worth. Balance is the layer where the user manually groups a cluster of Power notes into a MOC, once that cluster clearly represents one concept, theory, or school of thought — the grouping is a deliberate action, not something that emerges on its own. Development is what a Dataview query automatically assembles from every Balance MOC and Source note tagged to a discipline. Revolution is what the system alone cannot produce: an original synthesis, built by hand, that goes beyond what the links already show. Source sits outside this chain as the record of where every idea in the vault actually came from.

The long-term aim is a personal atlas of the humanities that is genuinely cross-disciplinary. A single idea, such as the concept of legitimacy, can live in Politics, History, Religion, and Philosophy at once without being copied four times, because Balance notes link outward to as many disciplines as they actually belong to. Over years, Development pages should come to reflect not a syllabus but the actual shape of what has been read, thought, and connected.

Words like "discipline" and "concept" describe the vault's original use, not a boundary on who it is for. The mechanism underneath — capture without judgment, structure only once earned, original work protected from automation — does not care whether the material behind it is a doctoral thesis, an exam next week, or a personal project with no institution attached to it at all. A Balance MOC named after a single assignment is exactly as legitimate as one named after a school of thought. The vocabulary is there to keep work findable, not to certify what kind of work is allowed to happen here.

## The Six Layers, in Practice

Essence holds no content notes. It is the vault's own constitution: the rules, the schema, the vocabulary, and this guide. Nothing about Art History or Philosophia belongs here.

Power is the entry point for everything. A note here can be a single sentence, a half-formed reaction to a book, or a paragraph copied from a lecture. Nothing is expected to be correct, complete, or well-organized. The only requirement is that it exists and is captured before it is lost.

Balance MOCs are created manually by the user, never generated by the system. A Balance note is a MOC, not a flat note: its `kind` field records whether it represents a concept, a theory, or a school of thought, and its `disciplines` field links it to one or more Development pages. The Power notes that belong to it are connected through ordinary wikilinks; a Dataview query then lists them automatically under "Linked Power Notes." There is no automatic trigger for creating a Balance MOC — the user decides when a set of Power notes is ready to be grouped this way, and creates the note from the Balance template.

Development is a set of discipline pages, one for each entry in the Disciplines vocabulary defined in [[System Rules]] — an open list, not a fixed count, since new disciplines may be documented and added as the user's work expands into them. These are not manually maintained lists; each one queries the vault for every Balance MOC and every Source linked to it, and displays them automatically. A Development page is a mirror of what the rest of the vault has built up around a discipline, not a hand-curated outline. Its *content* is never hand-maintained — the query fills it. But its *existence* is still a deliberate choice: a Balance MOC's `disciplines` field, or a Source's, can name a Development page that does not exist yet, and the user activates that page when the discipline actually becomes a focus of their study. What makes Development different from Balance is not the absence of a decision, but the absence of a *status threshold*: it does not wait for a status change the way a phantom Balance link waits for a Power note to reach `moved-to-balance`. The user may create the page the moment a discipline becomes a target, and once created it fills automatically from whatever already links to it. So Development is gated only by the choice to create it, never by an earned status. And until it is created, every Balance MOC and Source already tagged to that discipline stays fully reachable through its own links — activating the Development page marks the discipline as a focus, it does not unlock access to anything.

Revolution is deliberately outside the automatic pipeline. A Revolution MOC is built by hand, drawing on whatever the user chooses, in whatever shape the user chooses. This is where genuinely new connections are made, the ones that a purely bottom-up linking structure would never surface on its own. The template ships with no query, and none is ever added to it — but this does not mean Dataview is off-limits inside a Revolution note. A user can write their own query there, pulling together disciplines, sub-disciplines, or sources across boundaries that Development and Balance, each scoped to one discipline or concept, cannot cross on their own. What keeps this from becoming another automated layer is authorship: the system never supplies or suggests that query, so whatever it combines was a connection the user had already decided to make.

Revolution is also the one layer with no structural limit. Inside it, the user is free to build whatever arrangement the work demands — sub-folders for separate theses or articles, guiding MOCs that index other Revolution MOCs, any nesting the project calls for. A student can keep an undergraduate thesis, later graduate articles, and a personal project each in their own Revolution structure, all fed by the same Power–Balance–Development–Source layers underneath. The only rule is the negative one: whatever is built here must do something the other layers have not already done. Re-running a query Development already runs, or re-collecting what a Balance MOC already aggregates, is duplication, not Revolution. The freedom is total in shape and scope; the single constraint is that the work be genuinely the user's own, not a restatement of what the pipeline produced on its own.

Source is the bibliography of the vault. Every book, article, video, podcast, or course that feeds a Power or Balance note should have a Source entry, so that years later it is still clear where an idea originated. Each entry links to a broad discipline through `disciplines`, and where it feeds a specific Balance MOC, also through `subDisciplines` — so provenance can be traced not just to a field, but to the exact concept it informed. Source also holds a fourth kind of note that is not consumed material at all: Figures, people studied as subjects in their own right (this matters especially for History and Linguistics, where the person is as much the object of study as any text). A Source note points to the Figure(s) it is by or about through `relatedFigures`, and the Figure note's own query lists every Source pointing back to it automatically. Four fixed MOCs, one per category (Readable, Audible, Viewable, Figures), give a view of Source independent of discipline or project: what is queued, in progress, or finished, rated, or researched, useful both for auditing consumption and for spotting where to go next.

In fact, these layers are open to interpretation in completely different **ways and forms**. That is precisely why I avoided using **digital terminology** when naming these layers. The Balance layer can be thought of as a kind of **thesis and its sources**, while the **Development** layer represents the **antithesis**. The **Revolution** layer, in turn, can be viewed as the **synthesis**. The essence of the system is extremely open to being shaped and transformed according to the individual’s will and orientation.

## A Typical Workflow

Reading, watching, or listening to something new starts with a Source entry, created from the matching template in the Templates folder, so the material is logged before it is forgotten, with a `citationKey` assigned right away. As ideas come out of that material, they go into Power notes, quickly and without much editing. If a Power note is built around a specific passage rather than a general reaction, its `sourceRef` and `locator` fields point back to that Source note and the exact position within it (page, timestamp, or section), and the quote itself goes in the note body — so months later, when the idea resurfaces in an article or thesis draft, the citation is one click away instead of a re-read. When several Power notes start circling the same idea, that is the signal to activate a Balance MOC using the Balance template. The Balance MOC's disciplines field connects it to one or more Development pages; if a Development page for that discipline already exists, the Balance MOC appears on it automatically, and if it does not, the link waits as a phantom until the user activates that page — which they do once the discipline becomes a genuine focus of their study, not reflexively for every discipline a Balance MOC happens to touch. Once a Development page exists it needs no further action; it simply reflects what has accumulated. Periodically, when a genuinely original connection presents itself, a Revolution MOC can be built from scratch to capture it.

This describes the emergent path, where Revolution comes last, once something worth synthesizing has accumulated on its own. It is the natural rhythm for open-ended, curiosity-driven study. But it is not the only way to work, and for anyone with a defined goal — a thesis, an article, a blog post — the order can be inverted. Because Revolution has no activation threshold, its MOC may be opened at the very outset, alongside the first Source entries, and used as a declared target: a north star that the Power, Balance, and Development layers are then fed toward on purpose. Declaring the goal early is itself an act of authorship, not a violation of the earned principle — deciding what you are building is exactly the kind of judgment the system leaves to you. This goal-directed mode tends to be far more motivating than the emergent one, because the accumulation is organized by a visible destination from day one instead of waiting for a pattern to surface.

One caveat keeps the two modes honest: an early Revolution MOC is a target, not a shortcut. It does not exempt the work from passing through the real layers — sources are still logged, Power notes still captured, Balance MOCs still earned. The declared goal pulls the pipeline forward; it does not replace it. A Revolution note used to skip Power and Balance entirely is not goal-directed work, only unearned writing wearing the layer's name.

### AI and Tool Assistance
The vault imposes no restrictions on how material is captured or pre-processed. Using artificial intelligence or external tools to summarize sources, extract key concepts, or generate initial drafts inside Power notes is fully valid—it accelerates the intake of raw material. 

The single boundary lives at Revolution: automation may feed the intake, but it cannot substitute for human authorship. Inside Revolution, all structural scaffoldings drop away on one condition—that the work represents a genuine synthesis originated by the user's own curiosity and intent, producing something the automated pipeline could not have generated on its own.

**Tooling Freedom:** The architecture relies on data integrity, not specific plugins. While the vault ships with minimal dependencies to keep intake simple, you are encouraged to extend your workflow with plugins that fit your personal Obsidian practice.

## Using the Templates

Every note type in the vault has a template in the Templates folder: one for Power, one for Balance, one for Development, one for Revolution, nine for the different kinds of Source material, and one for Figures. New notes should be created with Command Palette, "Insert template", rather than typed from scratch, so the frontmatter schema stays consistent across the vault. The exact fields and their types are documented in [[Property Schema Reference]]; the allowed values for fields like status, category, and discipline, and the naming conventions, are documented in [[System Rules]].

## Automation

Several places in the vault use Dataview to reduce manual upkeep. Every Development page lists the Balance MOCs and Source notes connected to its discipline, generated live from the disciplines field, so nothing needs to be added by hand. Every Balance MOC lists the Power notes that link to it, generated live from ordinary wikilinks, and now also the Source notes that name it in their `subDisciplines` field. Every Figure note lists the Source notes that name it in their `relatedFigures` field, the same mirrored pattern. The four Source category MOCs list their category's notes by status, live. The Dashboard, in Essence, gives a single-page view of the vault's open work: Power notes not yet processed, Balance MOCs still in draft, Source material still to be read or watched, and Revolution MOCs still in progress.

Alongside Dataview, the vault's phantom links give a second, purely visual way to read the same signal. A phantom link is a link to a Balance MOC that does not exist yet — for example `[[Balance_Stoicism_MOC]]` written inside a Power note before that MOC has been created. Obsidian renders it as an unresolved link and leaves it that way for as long as every Power note pointing to it stays `raw` or `in-progress`; the MOC is only created, turning the phantom link real, once one of those Power notes reaches `moved-to-balance`. In the graph view, an unresolved node behaves the same as any other node: as more Power notes point to it, it visibly clusters and gathers connections before it is ever activated. This is not a query and produces no list, but for a user who thinks spatially rather than analytically, watching a cluster thicken around an unresolved node can surface the same "this is ready to become a MOC" signal that the Dashboard surfaces through `status = "raw"` and `status = "in-progress"` blocks. Neither view is required; a user may rely on the Dashboard, the graph, or both.

## What Success Looks Like

A vault where Power stays messy on purpose, where Balance MOCs are created only when the user has deliberately decided that a set of Power notes belongs together, not automatically and not prematurely, where Development pages read like an honest map of what has actually been studied rather than an aspirational one, and where Revolution notes are the clearest evidence, over time, of original thought rather than accumulation alone.
